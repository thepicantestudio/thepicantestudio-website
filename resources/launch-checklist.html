The launch content checklist

Seven things a product launch needs before day one, with a four-week countdown. One page. Tick it off.

By Raunak Singh Bhogal. Published 18 September 2026, updated 26 September 2026.

The short version

Most product launches go live missing at least three of these, and find out in launch week. Seven pieces of content, what each one is for, and what to ask a studio or a freelancer for so nothing gets remade later.

A hero film

Ten to fifteen seconds that make someone want the product with the sound off. Everything else in the launch is cut from it.

Ask for: 9:16 master, with and without text.

Listing stills

Clean pack shots on a plain ground for your site, marketplaces and quick commerce. Boring, and the images that actually sell.

Ask for: front, angle, scale shot, in-hand.

Every format

The same film in vertical, square and wide. Remaking an ad because it does not fit a placement is the most common launch-week panic.

Ask for: 9:16, 1:1, 16:9 from day one.

A page that matches

The ad makes a promise. The page they land on has to look like the same brand and say the same thing, or the click is wasted.

Check: same colours, same headline, one button.

Launch week, ready

Teaser, launch, proof, how to buy, reminder. Five posts written and designed before day one, not the night before.

Ask for: captions and covers with the files.

Three hooks

One product, three different opening lines. You will not guess which one works, so let the ads tell you in the first week.

Ask for: same film, three first-two-seconds.

One visual system

Colours, type and layout rules written down once. It is why some launches look like a brand and others look like ten freelancers.

Ask for: a one-page style sheet.

The four-week countdown

  • Four weeks out: the hero film in direction, listing stills briefed, the visual system written down.
  • Two weeks out: the film in every format, the landing page matching the ad, launch-week posts written.
  • One week out: three hook variants live for testing, captions and covers filed with the files, every placement checked on a real phone.

What you get

  • A four-week countdown: what to have ready four weeks, two weeks and one week out
  • The formats and sizes to ask for, so nothing gets remade later
